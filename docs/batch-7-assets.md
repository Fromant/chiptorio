# Batch 7 — cooling, quantum fabrication and network infrastructure

Candidate authoring, 2026-11-08. The next-batch request uses the square sizes
recorded in the machine art-direction plan: Cooling Tower 7×7, Quantum Fab
11×11, Switch and Router 3×3, Memory Bank 5×5. Installed footprints, recipes,
capacities, networking and power remain unchanged pending publication.

- Cooling Tower: twin induced-draft cells, exposed serpentine coils, pump skids,
  connected supply/return plumbing and counter-rotating fans.
- Quantum Fab: a stepped dilution refrigerator, cold-plate feed braids, an
  indexed substrate line, RF probe scan and a carrier test indicator. Loading,
  alignment, field work and unloading are marked on the Blender timeline.
- Network Switch: four-sided Ethernet port banks around an exposed switch ASIC.
- Network Router: a taller routing spine, uplink banks and redundant antennas.
- Memory Bank: three dense blade banks, edge connectors and a capacity bus.

Native crafting owns the cooling and quantum loops (192 source frames at 24 fps;
96 final frames at 12 fps). The three container devices use static artwork:
there is no new runtime event/activity owner or invented entity-state signal.
Fluid bindings preserve every original connection and its direction on the new
perimeter. All models are staged through Asset Studio and receive GPU-rendered
seven-camera inspections before review. No shipping files are installed here.

Inspection: Cycles HIP on the Radeon RX 9070 XT, CPU render devices disabled.
Game, front, left, right, rear, top and detail pose sheets were visually checked.
The Quantum Fab probe tip was raised to clear the substrate; the whole-cycle
moving-part scan reports no intersections. Cooling fan blades clear their guards;
the two reported spindle/hub contacts are intentional mounted joints. The three
static models were checked from the same views. Model build checks pass for loop
closure, floor/footprint envelope and zero idle emission. Exact `.blend` files and
diagnostic sheets are available in the one-at-a-time Model and motion queue.
